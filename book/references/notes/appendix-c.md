<a id="note-1"></a>

1. Lisa Feldman Barrett, “The Theory of Constructed Emotion: An Active Inference Account of Interoception and Categorization,” *Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience* 12, no. 1 (2017): 1–23, https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsw154. A category is “a population of events or objects that are treated as similar because they all serve a particular goal in some context,” and “the similarity need not be perceptual—it can be goal-based.”

<a id="note-2"></a>

2. Lisa Feldman Barrett, *How Emotions Are Made: The Secret Life of the Brain* (Boston: Houghton Mifflin Harcourt, 2017), chap. 5.

<a id="note-3"></a>

3. Lawrence W. Barsalou, “Ad Hoc Categories,” *Memory and Cognition* 11, no. 3 (1983): 211–27, at 223, 226, https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03196968. Barsalou writes that “a given entity can be cross-classified into an indefinitely large number of categories” (223) and that “a chair could subsequently be classified into ‘things that can be used for emergency firewood,’ ‘things that can be stood on,’ ‘gifts,’ and so on, depending on the current goal of the perceiver” (226).

<a id="note-4"></a>

4. Barsalou, “Ad Hoc Categories,” 211 (“People construct ad hoc categories to achieve goals”); Lawrence W. Barsalou, “Simulation, Situated Conceptualization, and Prediction,” *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B: Biological Sciences* 364, no. 1521 (2009): 1281–89, https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2008.0319, where “situated conceptualizations capture patterns of multi-modal simulation associated with frequently experienced situations” and current perception activates them.

<a id="note-6"></a>

6. My distinction parallels Barsalou's between context-independent properties, which “form the core meanings of words” and are “activated by the word for a concept on all occasions,” and context-dependent properties, which “are activated only by relevant contexts in which the word appears.” Lawrence W. Barsalou, “Context-Independent and Context-Dependent Information in Concepts,” *Memory and Cognition* 10, no. 1 (1982): 82–93, at 82, https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03197629. Barsalou addresses property activation, not identity conditions; treating the stable core as admissibility constraints is my own extension (see note 5).
