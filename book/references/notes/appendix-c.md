<a id="note-1"></a>

1. Lisa Feldman Barrett, “The Theory of Constructed Emotion: An Active Inference Account of Interoception and Categorization,” *Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience* 12, no. 1 (2017): 1–23, https://doi.org/10.1093/scan/nsw154. A category is “a population of events or objects that are treated as similar because they all serve a particular goal in some context,” and “the similarity need not be perceptual—it can be goal-based.”

<a id="note-2"></a>

2. Lisa Feldman Barrett, *How Emotions Are Made: The Secret Life of the Brain* (Boston: Houghton Mifflin Harcourt, 2017), chap. 5.

<a id="note-3"></a>

3. Lawrence W. Barsalou, “Ad Hoc Categories,” *Memory and Cognition* 11, no. 3 (1983): 211–27, at 223, 226, https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03196968. Barsalou writes that “a given entity can be cross-classified into an indefinitely large number of categories” (223) and that “a chair could subsequently be classified into ‘things that can be used for emergency firewood,’ ‘things that can be stood on,’ ‘gifts,’ and so on, depending on the current goal of the perceiver” (226).
